Method

Everything below is computed from the official GeoDAWN grids only. No catalogue, no label and no previously submitted raster enters the score.

1 · Inputs

  • Competition band 2, reduced-to-pole magnetic anomaly (nT) — official feature list.
  • Competition band 13, isostatic gravity anomaly (mGal).
  • Both are read at their native 100 m grid, EPSG:32611, 3730 × 3292, with the sentinel -1e38 mapped to NaN as the organiser reference solution does.

2 · Preparation

Nearest-neighbour gap fill inside the footprint, then a Gaussian band-pass (low-pass σ = 300 m, high-pass λ = 8 km) applied before differentiation, because the derivative operators amplify short-wavelength noise by |k| and |k|². Along-track median levelling was measured and switched off: the delivered grids show no flight-line striping (the standard deviation of the east–west mean profile is 24.10 against 24.70 for the north–south profile, ratio 0.976, and lag-1 autocorrelation is 0.992 along x against 0.995 along y). There is nothing to level, so levelling would only remove real signal.

3 · Pseudogravity

The magnetic anomaly is reduced to the pole, so magnetisation and inducing field are vertical and the anomaly is proportional to the vertical derivative of the magnetic scalar potential. The potential is recovered by dividing the spectrum by |k| (Baranov/Gunn pseudogravity), V̂ = T̂ / |k| with V̂(0) = 0. The gradient tensor is then built from V, so it is the Marussi tensor analysed by Pedersen & Rasmussen (1990) and Beiki & Pedersen (2010), not a third-derivative proxy. Eigenvalues and the horizontal strike azimuth are invariant to the sign convention chosen for the vertical operator, so the result does not depend on that choice.

4 · The tensor

Txx <-> -kx²      Tyy <-> -ky²      Txy <-> -kx·ky
Tzz = -(Txx+Tyy)  (Laplace: the tensor is traceless by construction)
Txz <-> i·kx·|k|   Tyz <-> i·ky·|k|

Closed-form symmetric 3×3 eigen-decomposition (Smith 1961) over all 12.28 M cells; verified against numpy.linalg.eigh in tests_numeric/test_core.py.

5 · Dimensionality and strike

e2 = λ1λ2 + λ1λ3 + λ2λ3      e3 = λ1λ2λ3
I  = 27·e3² / (4·(−e2)³)     0 = pure 2-D (λ = a, 0, −a);  1 = equidimensional 3-D

The intermediate eigenvalue's eigenvector gives the strike; its horizontality gives a plunge weight. The eigenvalue-ratio variant D = −λ2/λ1 is computed alongside and the holdout picks between them.

6 · Score

score = ridge · (1 − I) · exp(−(Δθ/25°)²) · plunge
ridge = sqrt(rank_mag · rank_grav)      (corroboration of both fields)
Δθ    = angle between the tensor strike and the ridge tangent (ridge tangent = gradient azimuth + 90°)

Ridges are the local maxima of the corroborated horizontal-gradient magnitude, thinned by non-maximum suppression to a minimum separation of 3 px — two dots closer than the 300 m kernel cannot both add true-positive credit (the metric takes a max), so packing them tighter only buys false positives.

7 · What was deliberately not done

  • No destriping (measured to be unnecessary — see §2).
  • No acquisition-block splitting: the delivered grid is a single merged, levelled product and neither official block polygons nor flight-line metadata are distributed with the competition files, so inventing them would be fabrication. The 64-pixel tiles used for amplitude equalisation are normalisation windows, not acquisition blocks, and are labelled as such.
  • No east–west mask: with no measurable striping, an E–W azimuth mask would delete real east-trending structures without removing an artefact. Measured, then dropped — reported rather than silently applied.
