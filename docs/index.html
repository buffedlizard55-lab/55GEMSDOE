🔬 Method: Tensor-Dimensionality Analysis

Why tensor dimensionality?

Gradient-ridge detectors can't distinguish a long fault from an intrusion — both produce ridges. The potential-field gradient tensor carries this information: its eigenvalues encode source dimensionality (0 for strike-extended faults, 1 for compact bodies), and eigenvectors carry strike direction.

Pipeline

  1. Field preparation: NaN-fill, destripe (remove flight-line artifacts), bandpass filter (300m–6000m wavelengths)
  2. Gradient tensor: FFT-based 2nd derivatives → 6-component symmetric tensor (Txx, Tyy, Txy, Tzz, Txz, Tyz)
  3. Eigenanalysis: Closed-form 3×3 eigendecomposition → dimensionality index + strike azimuth
  4. Gradient magnitude: Horizontal gradient |∇h| as primary edge detector
  5. Dimensionality gate: Weight = (1-dim)^0.5 — keeps faults, removes intrusions
  6. Cross-field corroboration: Geometric mean of magnetic and gravity responses
  7. NMS skeletonization: Non-maximum suppression thins to width-0 ridge crests
