The confirmatory test, preregistered and then run — it failed

NOT_SUPPORTED

The lane predicted that withheld faults' strikes should agree with the tensor strike more often than randomly chosen detected ridges do. The measurement goes the other way.

  • Group A — withheld catalogue fault segments (n = 1,661, ≥ 12 px): median angular difference 43.4°, 25.3% within 20°.
  • Group B — random detected ridges (n = 262, ≥ 600 m from any mapped fault): median 19.4°, 50.4% within 20°.
  • Two-proportion z = -8.35.

Group A's median of 43.4° is statistically indistinguishable from the 45° median of a uniform distribution on [0°, 90°]: at catalogue fault segments the tensor strike carries no information about the fault's own strike. Group B's low median is at least partly circular — the ridge azimuth and the tensor eigenvector come from the same field, so a strong ridge is self-consistent whether or not it is a fault. A negative result is a deliverable, so it is reported here and in run-card.json rather than dropped.

Why the holdout is not a score

HOLDOUT-DTI is measured against the public catalogue

The competition scores against new faults that are not in that catalogue. Every number above is a rank-ordering signal on a proxy task, not a prediction of the leaderboard value, and taking the holdout argmax as the budget would be over-fitting the proxy. The public-catalogue truth is 60,988 pixels against a private test set that the registry calibration puts at roughly 15,000–25,000 pixels, which is why the budget comes from the calibration and not from the sweep. The only organiser-confirmed number this project can hold is a submission receipt, and it has none.
